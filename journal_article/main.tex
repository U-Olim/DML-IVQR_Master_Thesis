\begin{abstract}
We study how weakening instrument relevance changes point accuracy, test calibration and inferential informativeness in high-dimensional instrumental variable quantile regression (IVQR). Extending the fixed-relevance Monte Carlo design of Chen, Huang, and Tien (2021), we scale the excluded signal and add independent compensating noise to preserve the joint distribution of treatment and the outcome disturbance. We compare preserved Oracle-GMM, Full-GMM and double/debiased machine learning IVQR (DML-IVQR) implementations across four relevance settings, five quantiles and sample sizes of 500 and 1000, with 500 replications per design; no new estimator or theorem is proposed. At $n=1000$, $\tau=0.50$ and $\kappa=0.10$, DML-IVQR has direct-at-truth acceptance $0.942$, a median grid-based accepted measure of $3.900$ out of $4$, and rejection probability $0.094$ at the false candidate $\alpha_0(\tau)+0.50$. These distinct measures show that nominal-looking truth acceptance can coexist with little exclusion of false effects; the acceptance frequency is not membership coverage of the stored finite-grid set. Oracle-GMM also deteriorates as relevance weakens. DML--Full RMSE rankings have exceptions, and substantial undercoverage occurs in several strong-relevance cells. Findings concern historical implementations with density proxies, differing residualization, no cross-fitting and numerical sensitivity documented in selected-case audits. Their nominal chi-square inference is not validated, and the comparisons do not isolate the effect of regularization.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} instrumental variable quantile regression; double machine learning; weak instruments; Monte Carlo simulation; confidence-set informativeness
