**Revised text**

```latex
We study how weakening instrument relevance changes point accuracy, test calibration and inferential informativeness in high-dimensional instrumental variable quantile regression (IVQR). Extending the fixed-relevance Monte Carlo design of Chen, Huang, and Tien (2021), we scale the excluded signal and add independent compensating noise to preserve the joint distribution of treatment and the outcome disturbance. We compare preserved Oracle-GMM, Full-GMM and double/debiased machine learning IVQR (DML-IVQR) implementations across four relevance settings, five quantiles and sample sizes of 500 and 1000, with 500 replications per design; no new estimator or theorem is proposed. At $n=1000$, $\tau=0.50$ and $\kappa=0.10$, DML-IVQR has direct-at-truth acceptance $0.942$, a median grid-based accepted measure of $3.900$ out of $4$, and rejection probability $0.094$ at the false candidate $\alpha_0(\tau)+0.50$. These distinct measures show that nominal-looking truth acceptance can coexist with little exclusion of false effects; the acceptance frequency is not membership coverage of the stored finite-grid set. Oracle-GMM also deteriorates as relevance weakens. DML--Full RMSE rankings have exceptions, and substantial undercoverage occurs in several strong-relevance cells. Findings concern historical implementations with density proxies, differing residualization, no cross-fitting and numerical sensitivity documented in selected-case audits. Their nominal chi-square inference is not validated, and the comparisons do not isolate the effect of regularization.
```

**Original text**

```latex
\section{Introduction}
Heterogeneous treatment effects motivate analysis across the potential-outcome distribution, but endogenous treatment prevents conventional quantile regression from recovering structural effects. Instrumental variable quantile regression (IVQR) combines excluded instruments with structural-rank restrictions to address this problem \parencite{koenker_bassett_1978,chernozhukov_hansen_2005}. In applications with many controls, nuisance estimation introduces an additional difficulty. Regularization and Neyman-orthogonal moments can reduce its impact on inference, but neither creates identifying information when instruments explain little treatment variation.

The IVQR literature distinguishes moment validity from identification and informative inference \parencite{chernozhukov_hansen_wuthrich_2017}. Inverse quantile regression provides practical estimation under regular identification \parencite{chernozhukov_hansen_2006}; candidate-specific test inversion accommodates weak identification under its maintained regularity conditions \parencite{chernozhukov_hansen_2008}. Its accepted regions can nevertheless be wide or disconnected. The finite-sample conditional procedure of \textcite{chernozhukov_hansen_jansson_2009} is a separate inferential construction. Related developments in the broader IV literature include the direct structural tests of \textcite{anderson_rubin_1949}, weak-IV approximations \parencite{staiger_stock_1997}, weak-identification-robust GMM \parencite{stock_wright_2000}, and pivotal and conditional likelihood-ratio procedures \parencite{kleibergen_2002,moreira_2003}. \textcite{andrews_stock_sun_2019} review their implications. Conventional first-stage diagnostics describe relevance, but do not provide universal quantile-specific IVQR identification thresholds.

High-dimensional regularization addresses a different source of difficulty. \textcite{belloni_chernozhukov_2011} develop penalized quantile regression and pivotal penalty calibration; \textcite{belloni_chernozhukov_kato_2019} establish orthogonal-score inference in approximately sparse quantile models. Double/debiased machine learning (DML) uses Neyman orthogonality to reduce first-order sensitivity to sufficiently small nuisance errors \parencite{chernozhukov_et_al_2018}. Cross-fitting is a distinct device for separating nuisance estimation from score evaluation. \textcite{chen_huang_tien_2021} combine regularized nuisance estimation and an orthogonal IVQR score, comparing Oracle-GMM, Full-GMM, and DML-IVQR in a high-dimensional Monte Carlo design. Further approaches address endogenous binary treatment \parencite{wuthrich_2019} and orthogonal moments for nonparametric IVQR with high-dimensional covariates \parencite{jin_sun_2025}.

The high-dimensional simulation in \textcite{chen_huang_tien_2021} holds instrument relevance fixed. We ask how progressively weakening that relevance changes point accuracy, coverage, power, and confidence-set informativeness across quantiles. Our contribution is a controlled Monte Carlo extension of their design and preserved executable procedures. A relevance parameter scales the excluded signal, while compensating independent noise preserves the treatment distribution and its joint distribution with the outcome disturbance. The extension introduces neither a new estimator nor a new inference theory.

The experiment compares three complete implementations on common samples. Oracle-GMM knows the designated relevant controls; Full-GMM uses all controls without regularization; DML-IVQR regularizes nuisance estimation. As relevance falls, all three lose point accuracy and numerical localization, while power generally declines. The preserved DML-IVQR implementation usually has lower RMSE than the preserved Full-GMM implementation, with exceptions. Coverage can approach 95\% as tests lose discrimination, and several strong-relevance cells exhibit substantial undercoverage. The implementation differences, non-finite population density weight, and asymptotic reference distribution qualify interpretation throughout. Deterioration of Oracle-GMM shows that high-dimensional nuisance estimation is not the sole explanation for the loss of accuracy. Differences in nuisance dimension, residualization and other computational details prevent attributing the DML--Full comparison to regularization alone.
```

Candidate-specific inversion accommodates weak identification under the conditions of the theoretical procedures \parencite{chernozhukov_hansen_2008}; weak relevance can nevertheless leave many false candidates unexcluded. Conventional first-stage diagnostics provide no universal IVQR identification threshold.

Many controls introduce a separate nuisance-estimation problem. Penalized quantile regression and orthogonal-score methods reduce sensitivity to nuisance errors under their respective conditions \parencite{belloni_chernozhukov_2011,belloni_chernozhukov_kato_2019,chernozhukov_et_al_2018}, but do not create identifying variation. \textcite{chen_huang_tien_2021} combine regularized nuisance estimation and an orthogonal IVQR score in a high-dimensional Monte Carlo design. Their theoretical construction motivates this study; our empirical comparisons concern preserved executable implementations.

The high-dimensional simulation of \textcite{chen_huang_tien_2021} holds instrument relevance fixed. It does not trace performance as relevance weakens while preserving the treatment distribution and its relationship with the outcome disturbance. We address this gap by scaling the excluded signal and adding independent compensating noise. Under the stated Gaussian construction, the modification preserves the joint distribution of treatment and the outcome disturbance while changing treatment--instrument relationships. This controls treatment dispersion, without isolating computational mechanisms behind estimator differences.

The scientific question extends beyond point accuracy. Direct-at-truth acceptance can approach the nominal confidence level while most of the grid is retained and few false candidates are rejected; a more accurate point estimate also need not imply better calibration. Reported coverage is a direct-test acceptance diagnostic, not literal membership coverage of stored grid sets. Fresh pivotal draws can distinguish separate DML evaluations even at the same candidate; unrestricted inversion remains conceptual.

The paper makes three empirical contributions. First, it specifies a controlled Monte Carlo relevance modification preserving the stated treatment/endogeneity distribution. Second, it compares preserved Oracle-GMM, Full-GMM and DML-IVQR implementations across relevance settings and quantiles on common observed samples. Third, it jointly evaluates point accuracy, direct-at-truth acceptance, false-candidate rejection and finite-grid informativeness. No new estimator or inference theorem is proposed.

The preserved DML-IVQR implementation has lower RMSE than the preserved Full-GMM implementation in 35 of the 40 matched designs, with documented exceptions. Differences in nuisance dimension, residualization and computational details prevent attributing this comparison to regularization alone. Oracle-GMM also deteriorates under weaker relevance, so high-dimensional nuisance estimation is not the sole explanation. Strong-relevance undercoverage limits any reading of nominal-looking acceptance as validated inference.

The findings concern one DGP family and historical implementations using density proxies, differing residualization, same-sample nuisance fits and fresh penalty draws. The original DGP has a non-finite population density weight. Implemented orthogonality, validity of the nominal chi-square cutoff and transfer of theoretical weak-identification robustness remain unestablished; absence of cross-fitting alone does not prove invalidity. Selected-case audits document residual-rank and decision sensitivity, not a full-experiment error frequency (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}). Historical results are retained, and finite-grid diagnostics establish no unrestricted confidence-set geometry.
